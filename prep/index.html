How NeuroPrep trains your brain

Eight evidence-based cognitive strategies

01

Spaced Repetition

Items return at expanding intervals calibrated to your forgetting curve (Ebbinghaus, 1885). Each successful recall extends the interval exponentially.

02

Active Recall

You answer before seeing the explanation. Retrieval effort strengthens memory traces more than passive re-reading (Roediger & Karpicke, 2006).

03

Interleaving

Mixed-subject sessions prevent category-cue shortcuts and force discrimination between concepts (Kornell & Bjork, 2008).

04

Elaborative Interrogation

Explanations prompt you to connect new facts to existing knowledge, building richer associative networks (Pressley et al., 1987).

05

Leitner System

Questions move between five boxes based on accuracy. Wrong answers return to Box 1. Mastered items graduate to Box 5 for long-term maintenance.

06

Desirable Difficulty

Sessions are deliberately challenging — the productive struggle of effortful retrieval produces deeper encoding (Bjork & Bjork, 2011).

07

Confidence Calibration

Rating your certainty reveals overconfidence biases. Metacognitive awareness improves judgment accuracy (Dunlosky & Rawson, 2012).

08

Concept Mapping

The knowledge graph visualises how topics connect. Seeing the structure of your knowledge reveals gaps and strengthens transfer (Novak & Cañas, 2008).
